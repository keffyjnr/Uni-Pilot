About the Department

Department information based on AAUA's official Mathematical Sciences page.

Welcome to Mathematics at AAUA. Mathematics provides a symbolic language for describing relationships, solving problems and predicting outcomes. It supports reasoning and decision-making across scientific, business and other practical settings.

Programme overview: The B.Sc. Mathematics programme develops mathematical knowledge, logical reasoning, analytical thinking and problem-solving skills. Mathematics and Industrial Mathematics are offered within the Department of Mathematical Sciences; Uni Pilot presents them as separate guides to make each programme easier for students to find.

Academic focus: Students build a foundation in mathematical concepts and methods, with opportunities to understand both theoretical mathematics and its applications. Refer to the current departmental handbook for the approved curriculum and programme-specific requirements.

LEARNING & DEVELOPMENT

Programme Objectives & Skills

  • Provide broad and balanced biochemical knowledge and practical skills.
  • Apply knowledge to theoretical and practical problems in mathematics.
  • Develop transferable skills for biochemical and non-biochemical employment.
  • Prepare students for further study in specialised and multidisciplinary areas.
  • Show the value of interdisciplinary approaches to complex life problems.
  • Appreciate mathematics's role in industrial, economic, environmental, technological and social development.
Unique programme features
  • Develops cognitive abilities in mathematics and related sciences.
  • Introduces tropical flora and fauna and potential uses in drug development.
  • Builds laboratory and instrumentation skills.
  • Develops scientific information literacy and independent learning.
  • Strengthens critical thinking for industrial, environmental and life-science problems.
Employability skills
  • Critical thinking, problem-solving, analysis and interpretation.
  • Creativity, innovation and personal expression.
  • Research, scientific questioning and inquiry.
  • Oral and written communication.
  • ICT literacy and data analysis.
  • Understanding of industrial biochemical processes.
  • Entrepreneurship and small/medium enterprise development.

ENTRY & GRADUATION

Admission Requirements

Confirm current Mathematics-specific requirements with AAUA Admissions before applying.

UTME Entry · 100 Level

Applicants generally need five relevant O’Level credit passes, including English Language and Mathematics, and must meet the university’s current UTME and screening requirements.

Subject combination: Check the current JAMB brochure and AAUA admission notice for the approved Mathematics combination for the session.

Direct Entry · 200 Level

Direct Entry applicants should check the current AAUA and JAMB requirements for acceptable A-Level, JUPEB or equivalent qualifications in Mathematics and other relevant subjects.

Graduation Requirements

Students must complete and pass the approved programme’s compulsory courses, required electives and other university requirements. The current Mathematics handbook should be used to confirm minimum credit units and degree regulations.
