About the Department

Agricultural Economics

The Department of Agricultural Economics is domiciled in the Faculty of Agriculture at Adekunle Ajasin University. The B.Sc. Agricultural Economics programme combines economic theory with practical agricultural knowledge to prepare graduates to address food security, agricultural productivity, sustainable food systems and economic resilience.

📍 View Department Location

Programme Philosophy, Vision & Objectives

Programme Direction

Philosophy

The B.Sc. Agricultural Economics programme seeks to equip students with analytical skills, an innovative mindset and a global perspective to address food-security challenges. It blends economic theory with practical agricultural insights and prepares graduates for work in academia, research, policy-making and the agricultural industry. Graduates are expected to apply evidence-based approaches to improve productivity, reduce food waste and develop resilient food supply chains in support of food security and sustainable development.

Vision

To develop globally competitive agricultural economists who drive sustainable food systems, enhance productivity and lead innovative solutions for food security and economic resilience worldwide.

Objectives

  • Develop a skilled workforce with the technology and knowledge needed for productive rural agricultural settings.
  • Support national self-sufficiency in essential food and agricultural raw materials for industrialisation.
  • Modernise agricultural production, processing, storage, preservation and distribution.
  • Expand employment opportunities and improve quality of life in rural communities.
  • Adapt and apply external technologies to local agricultural management challenges.
  • Encourage graduates to treat farming as a viable enterprise and exercise sound professional judgement.
  • Prepare manpower for agricultural production and research across relevant sectors.

Unique Features of the Programme

  • Aligns with the National Policy on Agriculture and addresses theoretical and practical priorities across local, national and global agricultural value chains.
  • Uses SMART objectives: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Realistic and Time-bound.
  • Supports career pathways in agricultural communications, social services, production, finance, sales and marketing.
  • Prepares graduates for opportunities in agro-processing, agro-allied industries and agri-finance, as well as further study.
  • Builds specialist knowledge for advanced study and practical problem-solving.

Employability Skills

  • Self-employment and job creation as agricultural economists or agribusiness enterprise owners.
  • Professional services in agribusiness, agro-allied industries and agricultural finance.
  • Manpower for agro-industrial manufacturing.
  • Roles in agro-allied, marketing and financial sectors.
  • Collaboration with other specialist sectors to support economic growth and development.

21st-Century Skills

  • Critical thinking and creativity
  • Collaboration and communication
  • Information, media and technology literacy
  • Flexibility and adaptability
  • Leadership and initiative
  • Productivity and social skills

Admission

Admission Requirements

UTME Entry Requirements

In addition to an acceptable UTME score, candidates must have at least five SSCE credit passes, including English Language, Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry and Biology or Agricultural Science, at not more than two sittings.

Direct Entry Requirements

For the three-year Direct Entry route, candidates must have five SSCE credit passes including English Language, plus two principal passes at Advanced GCE Level or NCE (or equivalent). Holders of OND, NCE, JUPEB or IJMB with credit passes in relevant subjects may be considered for 200 Level entry.

UTME Subject Combination

English Language, Mathematics or Physics, Chemistry, and Biology or Agricultural Science. Candidates should confirm the current approved subject combination with the university and JAMB before applying.

Programme Duration

UTME: four academic sessions (four years). Direct Entry: three academic sessions, with admission normally into 200 Level, subject to applicable requirements.

Graduation

Graduation Requirements

Students must complete and pass the approved compulsory and elective courses and satisfy the university's other graduation conditions.

Course Registration Guidance

Semester Allocation

Course codes ending in an odd number are placed under First Semester (for example, AGR 101). Course codes ending in an even number are placed under Second Semester (for example, AGR 102).

Course Status

C indicates a compulsory course and E indicates an elective course. Where an elective choice is specified, students should select the required number of options in line with departmental guidance.
