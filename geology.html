Welcome & Background

About the Department

Welcome to Geology at AAUA. This page focuses on the B.Sc. (Hons.) Geology programme and presents the course structure supplied in the 2025/2026 Faculty Brochure.

Department background: The Department of Earth Sciences was formerly the Department of Geology. It was established in 1982 at Ondo State University, Ado-Ekiti, and relocated to Akungba-Akoko in December 1999. Applied Geophysics was introduced as a separate degree programme. Senate approved the name Department of Earth Sciences on 27 November 2013.

Both Geology and Applied Geophysics are reported in the supplied brochure as NUC-accredited/verified and COMEG-certified programmes.

Programme Philosophy & Objectives

What the Programme Aims to Develop

Programme Philosophy

To develop enthusiasm and practical skills in geological studies, problem-solving in theoretical and field geology, and preparation for work in petroleum, groundwater, mineral prospecting, civil engineering and environmental sectors.

Programme Objectives

  • Build strong scientific and practical skills.
  • Develop field, laboratory, analytical and problem-solving abilities.
  • Prepare students for research, further study and relevant geoscience careers.
  • Develop competence in interpreting Earth materials and processes or geophysical data, according to the programme.
Admission

Admission Requirements

Transcribed from the supplied 2025/2026 Faculty Brochure. Confirm current requirements with AAUA and JAMB before applying.

UTME Entry

  • Five O’Level credit passes at not more than two sittings: English Language, Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry and Biology.
  • UTME subjects: English, Chemistry and Physics, plus Biology or Mathematics.

Direct Entry

  • Advanced-level passes in at least two of Chemistry, Mathematics, Biology and Physics; or
  • ND in relevant Geosciences with Upper Credit.

Programme Duration & Credit Requirements

UTME/Pre-Degree: 4 years (8 semesters), minimum 120 credit units. Direct Entry: 3 years (6 semesters), minimum 90 credit units. The brochure states 15–28 credit units per semester and at least 30 credit units per session.

Academic Regulations

Examination, Grading & Degree Classification

Assessment

Continuous assessment contributes 30% and the final semester examination contributes 70%. Final-year project scripts and oral examinations are moderated or conducted by external examiners.
