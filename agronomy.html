Introduction & General Background

Department of Agronomy

The crop science programme curriculum is designed to impact knowledge in a broad sense in all aspects of crop science, production, produce preservation and utilization. It emphasizes a complete value chain in crop production.

As much as possible, it teaches the deployment of modern technology in meeting the emerging challenges of food insecurity and grooming learners for the development of a sustainable enterprise. An appropriate delivery of the contents of the course modules of this curriculum would produce not just scientists, but equally entrepreneurs in crop production, value addition to crop produce as well as packaging of final crop produce.

Programme Philosophy & Objectives

What the Programme Aims to Develop

Philosophy

The principal focus of the national agriculture policy is to strengthen the component parts of the agricultural sector, with particular reference to the crop production sub-sector to meet the demand for food and industrial raw materials. Thus, the philosophy and mission statement underlying the crop science programme of agriculture are aimed at making a positive and significant contribution to the nation's gross domestic product (GDP), and furthering Nigeria's renewed commitment to food sufficiency and general self-reliance through graduates equipped with comprehensive theoretical knowledge and practical skills for meaningful engagement in crop production.

Objectives

The objectives of the programmes in agriculture are in tandem with national needs and priorities. Graduates are expected to be resourceful, creative and knowledgeable in solving relevant societal problems.

  1. Equip graduates with adequate knowledge in the theory and practice of crop production to meet increasing food demand, food security and self-sufficiency.
  2. Enhance national industrialization through increased production of industrial raw materials for a diversified economy.
  3. Advance knowledge in modern production techniques, produce processing and value addition through processing, storage, preservation, transit and distribution.
  4. Improve employability and employment opportunities, reducing social ills resulting from unemployment.
  5. Apply contemporary technology to improve crop production techniques and productivity per land area.
  6. Produce graduates confident in entering practical crop-production enterprises.
  7. Equip graduates with scientific knowledge and background for effective research in crop science.
Programme Features & Skills

Employability and 21st Century Skills

Employability Skills

The curriculum takes due cognizance of employability, developing competence and skills in:

  • Production enterprise in arable crop farming.
  • Production enterprise in vegetable crop farming.
  • Production enterprise in orchard establishment and management.
  • Enterprise in seed production, processing and packaging.
  • Consultancy in all aspects of crop production.
  • Research in crop science and crop improvement.

21st Century Skills

The new CCMAS addresses emerging 21st-century skill gaps in crop science, including:

  • Critical thinking, problem solving, reasoning, analysis, interpretation and synthesizing information.
  • Research skills and practices, including interrogative questioning.
  • Leadership, teamwork and collaboration.
  • Learning skills and literacy skills.
  • Oral and written communication and public speaking.
  • Perseverance, self-direction, planning, self-discipline, adaptability and initiative.
  • ICT literacy, media and internet literacy, and computer programming.

Unique Features of the Programme

  • The new crop science programme's philosophy and objectives align with the goals of the national policy on agriculture, making graduates relevant to national and global agricultural development.
  • The programme provides broad training at lower levels, giving students basic knowledge across agriculture before advanced crop science training in later years.
  • Practical training is enterprise-based and addresses food and industrial crop supply gaps, contributing to food security.
Programme Offered & Duration

B. Agric. (Honours) in Agronomy

🎓

Bachelor of Agriculture Degree

The programme leads to the B. Agric. (Honours) degree in Agronomy.

⏱️

Duration

UTME candidates spend a minimum of four academic sessions (four years). Direct Entry candidates admitted to 200 level spend a minimum of three academic sessions (three years).

Admission

Admission Requirements

UTME — Four Year Degree Programme

In addition to the UTME score, candidates should possess five credit passes in Senior Secondary Certificate (SSC), including English Language, Mathematics, Biology or Agricultural Science, Chemistry and Physics, in not more than two sittings.

Direct Entry Mode

Candidates must have at least two Advanced Level credit passes or equivalent in Chemistry and Biology, plus the five SSC (or equivalent) credit passes prescribed for UTME entry. Diploma holders with a minimum of Lower Credit, plus the five SSC (or equivalent) credit passes, obtained in not more than two sittings, are eligible for consideration for admission into 200 level.

Graduation

Graduation Requirements

To graduate, a student shall have completed four years of study depending on the entry point, including one practical year or six months of practicals. Activities during the practical period should include periodic seminars on the student's work and the presentation of a written report for grading at the end of the period.

Candidates admitted through UTME must register for at least 120 course units for the four-year track, while Direct Entry candidates must register for at least 84 units for the three-year track, as indicated under course requirements. Candidates must also register and pass all compulsory courses specified for the programme.

Submission of an undergraduate project report based on supervised research is a graduation requirement. It exposes students to problem-solving techniques and develops their ability to organize ideas from literature and research findings, preparing them for post-graduation careers and further postgraduate training.
