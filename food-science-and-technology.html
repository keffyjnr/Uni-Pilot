Introduction & General Background

Food Science and Technology

Welcome to the Department of Food Science and Technology, Faculty of Agriculture, Adekunle Ajasin University, Akungba-Akoko. The Department is committed to developing knowledgeable, skilled and innovative graduates who can contribute to food production, processing, preservation, quality assurance and the advancement of the food industry.

Food Science and Technology is an interdisciplinary field applying scientific and engineering principles to food processing, preservation, packaging, storage, distribution, analysis and evaluation. It examines the physical, chemical, biological and nutritional properties of food and develops ways to improve food quality, safety, availability and consumer acceptability.

The programme addresses food security and post-harvest challenges by preparing graduates to transform agricultural raw materials into safe, nutritious and marketable food products. It combines theoretical instruction with practical learning, laboratory experiments, food analysis, processing activities and product development.

Programme Philosophy, Aim & Objectives

What the Programme Aims to Develop

Philosophy

The programme responds to the need for skilled manpower in preserving agricultural crops and developing food products and processes that provide nutritious and balanced diets. It applies science and engineering to food processing, preservation, packaging, storage, distribution, analysis and the design and evaluation of food systems and machinery.

Its philosophy is to produce graduates with strong academic standards and practical competence in food science and post-harvest management, prepared for self-employment and valuable service to industry, academia and the wider community in support of global food security.

Aim

The programme aims to support the National Policy on Agriculture and Nigeria's commitment to food sufficiency and self-reliance. It prepares graduates with theoretical knowledge and practical skills for meaningful work in food management, processing and technology, enabling them to become self-reliant and valuable contributors to industry and society.

Programme Objectives

  1. Train skilled technical and managerial manpower for agro-allied industries.
  2. Prepare graduates to establish, manage and direct small-scale food-processing and related industries using locally available raw materials.
  3. Develop personnel for consultancy, product development, research, processing and preservation to reduce post-harvest losses.
  4. Equip graduates with knowledge of food-processing equipment design and fabrication and the development of traditional technology.
  5. Prepare graduates for postgraduate study and teaching in Food Science and Technology and related disciplines.
  6. Contribute to national self-sufficiency in basic food and agricultural raw materials needed for industrialisation.
  7. Support modernisation of agricultural production, processing, storage, preservation and distribution.
  8. Improve employment opportunities and contribute to rural development and quality of life.
  9. Develop professional judgement and entrepreneurial ability to establish small- and medium-scale enterprises.
  10. Produce manpower for research and for production, quality assurance and quality-control management in food-processing industries.
Training & Career Development

Learning, Practical Training & Employability

Food Sector Skills

  • Food quality management, quality assurance and quality control.
  • Food safety management, hygiene, legislation and control.
  • Research and development, product development, consumer and nutritional science.
  • Food production operations, manufacturing management, engineering maintenance, health, safety and environmental practices.
  • Food retail, supply-chain logistics, transportation and food-service support.
  • Food processing and manufacturing, including meat and poultry processing.

Employability & Career Opportunities

  • Research and development officer in food industries.
  • Food production, processing and quality assurance/control manager.
  • Consultant assessing food-processing plants and recommending good manufacturing practices.
  • Entrepreneur establishing cottage or small-scale food-processing businesses.
  • Food inspection and regulatory roles with agencies such as NAFDAC and the Standards Organisation of Nigeria.
  • Food-processing equipment design and construction.
  • Teaching and research in appropriate institutions.
  • Opportunities with food and agriculture organisations, including FAO, subject to recruitment requirements.

Five 21st-Century Priority Skills

  1. Product development.
  2. Food legislation and control, and food safety management.
  3. Food hygiene and food safety control.
  4. Quality management, quality assurance and quality control.
  5. Research.

Admission

Admission Requirements

UTME Entry Requirements

  1. Meet the University's UTME admission conditions.
  2. Obtain at least five SSCE credit passes, including English Language, Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry, and Biology or Agricultural Science.
  3. The five required credits must be obtained in not more than two sittings.

UTME subject combination: English Language, Mathematics or Physics, Chemistry, and Biology or Agricultural Science.

Direct Entry Requirements

  1. Five SSCE credit passes, including English Language, plus two principal passes in relevant subjects at Advanced GCE, NCE or equivalent level.
  2. OND, NCE, JUPEB or IJMB holders with credit passes in relevant subjects may be considered for admission into 200 Level.
  3. HND holders with Upper Credit may be considered for admission into 300 Level.

Note: The supplied text also mentions OND/HND or equivalent with at least Lower Credit for advanced placement. Confirm the exact entry level and minimum qualification with AAUA Admissions.

Graduation

Graduation Requirements

Students must complete and pass the approved compulsory and elective courses, meet the minimum credit-unit requirements for their entry route, and satisfy departmental and University regulations.

  • UTME students: minimum of 182 credit units.
  • Direct Entry students: minimum of 156 credit units.
  • Complete all compulsory courses and required elective units.
  • Obtain a minimum CGPA of 1.50.
  • Meet any additional requirements prescribed by the Department and Senate.

Registration

Course Status & Registration Guidance

Course Categories

Compulsory (C): Core courses that must be taken and passed.

Required (R): Courses that must be taken and passed.

Elective (E): Approved options students may select to meet the minimum credit-unit requirement.

University Courses: University courses are treated as required courses.

Course Registration

Register for courses according to the approved level and semester structure, entry route and departmental guidance. The tables preserve the First Semester and Second Semester arrangement printed in the programme document.

Confirm elective choices and course-code or title discrepancies with the Department before registration.

Assessment

Evaluation of Students' Performance

Course Assessment

  • Each course is graded out of 100 marks.
  • Continuous assessment accounts for 30%.
  • The end-of-course examination accounts for 70%.
  • The minimum pass mark is 40%.

Academic Standing & GPA

GPA is calculated by multiplying each course's grade point by its credit units, adding the weighted points for the semester and dividing by the total registered credit units. CGPA is calculated by dividing cumulative weighted grade points across semesters by the total credit units registered.

Academic status codes: P — Probation; WN — Warning at the end of the semester after probation where GPA is below 1.50; W — Withdrawal from course.
